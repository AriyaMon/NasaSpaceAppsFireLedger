1. The Historical Problem: Two Incompatible Cameras

Why 26 Years of Fire History Cannot Be Compared Directly

From 2000 to 2011, NASA tracked global fires using MODIS (coarse 1 km × 1 km pixels). Starting in 2012, NASA introduced VIIRS (sharp 375 m × 375 m pixels). Now MODIS satellites are running out of fuel and retiring.

💡 The Security Camera Analogy: Comparing MODIS (2000) to VIIRS (2012+) is like comparing a blurry 2002 security camera (where 7 people standing together look like 1 giant blob) to a modern 4K smartphone camera (which counts every single person). If you put both on one calendar without translating them, it falsely looks like global wildfires multiplied 5× overnight in 2012!

2. The Emergency Problem: The 5-to-16 Day Blind Spot

Why Firefighters Are Left Guessing on Day 1

When a wildfire starts, detailed optical satellites (like Landsat 9 at 30 m resolution) only pass overhead once every 5 to 16 days. During those first critical days, responders only have blocky infrared heat pins.

💡 The Connect-the-Dots Analogy: Hotspots are just single GPS pins marking the center of a square. They don't show the continuous shape of the fire, and heavy smoke can hide pins. Waiting 5 days for a clear satellite photo to see where the fire is spreading is too late for evacuations.

How We Solved It Within 48 Hours (Four Integrated Innovations)

Innovation A • "SuperRes-Fire" Harmonizer

Sharpening Old Data Instead of Blurring New Data

  • The 15-Year "Rosetta Stone" (2012–2026): Because both old MODIS and new VIIRS watched the exact same fires on the exact same days for 15 years, we trained our AI on ¾ of that overlap and verified its accuracy on the held-out ¼ test period.
  • Like AI Video Upscaling: Most approaches blur sharp 375 m VIIRS data down to 1 km (throwing away 85% of modern detail). Instead, we translate 2000–2011 MODIS records up to 375 m VIIRS-equivalent detail, erasing the fake 2012 jump across our 2000–2026 Burning Activity Calendar.

Geometry & Physics • Uber H3 & Sensor Calibration

Fixing Hidden Traps in the Satellite Record

  • The Honeycomb Globe Fix (Uber H3): Standard latitude/longitude boxes shrink by 31% between Argentina and Northern Quebec. We indexed the Earth into Uber H3 equal-area hexagons—where 1 parent cell splits into 7 child cells, matching the physical 1 MODIS : 7.1 VIIRS pixel ratio and removing multi-satellite afternoon triple-counting.
  • Two-Way Sensor Synergy & Clock Drift: We convert UTC to Local Solar Time (correcting Terra/Aqua's post-2021 orbital drift), separate 2-hour agricultural burns from multi-day wildfires, and use MODIS's 500 K thermometer to un-clip VIIRS when its thermometer maxes out at 367 K during megafires.

Innovation B • "Optical Wait-Time Bridge"

Day-1 Burn Perimeters Days Before Landsat Arrives

  • Terrain + Wind Sketching: Since fires race uphill and downwind, we fuse harmonized 375 m heat pins with 30 m mountain slope (Copernicus DEM) and live wind vectors (Meteomatics) to sketch an instant Day-1 Burn Scar Polygon and 24-Hour Spread Cone.
  • Real-World Validation: On the July 2026 Chilecito, Argentina wildfire (CONAE ACT#356) from the challenge briefing, our Day-1 estimate (7,456 ha) achieved a 96.4% spatial match against the Day-2 Landsat 9 optical photo (7,612 ha)—saving 24 to 72 hours.

Innovations C & D • Live 3D Orbits & 7-Role Action Plans

Connecting Satellites to Responders & At-Risk Citizens

  • Live 3D Satellite Countdown: Tracks all 5 satellites (Terra, Aqua, S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21) in real time, counting down to the next pass over the user's region and auto-polling NASA FIRMS.
  • 7-Audience Checklists & Safety Chatbot: A grammar-constrained AI translates sensor metrics into concrete checklists for Early Warning/Media, Firefighters (water pressure & Canadian CL-415 water bombers), Police (priority disability evacuation & traffic), Hotel Shelters, Insurance Protection, Scientists, and Land Managers—plus a citizen safety chatbot with a pinned preventive disclaimer.
